Process snapshot · 11 August 2026

Newsroom system map

From signal to finished edition.

A living map of the evidence-first production process: what the system must do, what currently works, where human judgment enters, and what remains to be built.

Current working edge

Feed-gated Calibre collection, canonical Rails/SQLite persistence, local clustering, deterministic dossier creation, and schema-constrained editorial scoring now work as bounded experiments for a United States audience.

The daily evidence window is locked to 2:00 AM cutoff-to-cutoff in America/New_York, with a 5:00 AM weekday publication. Monday spans Friday 2:00 AM through Monday 2:00 AM. New evidence may attach to active clusters from prior editions; unchanged clusters are not rescored.

Status language

Working experiment In progress Not built Decision required

“Working” means demonstrated in the current prototype. It does not mean production-approved, deployed, or editorially validated at scale.

End-to-end status

The production line

The sequence preserves a strict boundary between source material, verified claims, original writing, and publication. A green technical call never substitutes for an editorial gate.

  1. In progress

    Source policy & registration

    Decide what may be collected and how it may be used.

    The Federal Register and NewsAPI.ai experiments are preserved. A strict per-publisher manifest now declares identity, recipe, feed, allowed hosts, update semantics, operational limits, and a draft policy proposal. Agents cannot approve their own sources. PBS NewsHour and The Marshall Project need extraction cleanup; ABC News and Christian Science Monitor remain discovery-only after terms review. All policies remain draft.

    ArtifactVersioned SourcePolicy and source register entry.

  2. Working experiment

    Discover & collect

    Obtain candidate events and permitted source material.

    The original ten-source Calibre run produced 653 schema-valid records and 606 unique URLs. A new wrapper now filters parse_index() and parse_feeds() results before Calibre schedules downloads. The first run fetches unseen articles; an unchanged second run still checks discovery but requests zero known article pages. In-run URL duplicates are removed before fetching.

    Artifactcalibre-known-state/v1, discovery-decision JSONL, normalized article JSONL, run report, and ignored per-source live evidence.

  3. Working experiment

    Normalize & store articles

    Create stable inputs before clustering or scoring.

    The production path now targets the canonical Rails database rather than the separate experimental corpus. SourceDocument stores stable identity and distinct publication, publisher-update, and trusted discovery-marker times. Append-only DocumentSnapshot records retain title-plus-body versions; discovery observations retain every fetch/skip decision. Leased hourly slots prevent duplicate or overlapping source runs and permit explicit recovery after a crashed lease.

    ArtifactRails SQLite source_documents, document_snapshots, discovery_observations, collection_runs, and news_collection_slots.

  4. Working experiment

    Form event clusters

    Group coverage of the same real-world episode.

    The accepted experimental baseline embeds the 552-article frozen corpus with pinned BGE-small and applies average-linkage agglomerative clustering at cosine distance 0.145. It produced 53 clusters containing 146 articles; 42 clusters span multiple publishers. Manual review found strong same-event groups and a small number of tolerable false joins. The broader 0.200 view visibly overmerged topics and remains diagnostic only.

    ArtifactIgnored JSON result, embeddings, and HTML cluster-review report with pairwise similarities and nearest excluded articles.

  5. Working experiment

    Build a ranking dossier

    Prepare bounded, relevant evidence for one event.

    For a local cluster, deterministic code excludes bodies under 150 words, chooses one representative per publisher by within-cluster centrality, and includes no more than six sources. A substantial singleton is valid evidence; publisher count is reported to the model rather than used as an exclusion rule.

    ArtifactStructured dossier and Markdown prompt containing event metadata, precomputed coverage, source provenance, and bounded article evidence.

  6. Working experiment

    Classify & score importance

    Apply editorial dimensions consistently across events.

    A strict OpenRouter JSON-schema call returns section, five scores, confidence, and a concise reason. A three-case local-corpus test separated a seven-publisher war cluster, a consequential United States singleton, and a low-stakes culture singleton without using article count as an importance shortcut.

    ArtifactValidated score record; no publication decision.

  7. Not built

    Rank & select clusters

    Compare candidates within sections and a finite edition budget.

    The intended direction is deterministic comparison of model-produced dimensions, selecting the top candidates per category and then cutting to the final reading-length budget. The formula, category quotas, tie-breaking, and diversity rules are undecided.

    ArtifactProposed story list with inclusion rationale.

  8. Not built

    Extract atomic claims

    Turn source material into independently reviewable assertions.

    Every claim must retain evidence, attribution, time, and one of four explicit states: confirmed, attributed, disputed, or unknown. Article prose must not become the writer’s narrative template.

    ArtifactClaim and claim-level EvidenceItem records.

  9. Not built

    Resolve evidence & uncertainty

    Compare accounts before prose is written.

    Contradictions, single-source claims, stale evidence, original-reporting attribution, and material unknowns must be surfaced. Primary sources should be added where available; unsupported inference cannot silently become fact.

    ArtifactReviewed claim set with warnings and unresolved questions.

  10. Not built

    Draft the story

    Write original inverted-pyramid reporting from approved claims.

    The writer receives claims, evidence references, required attribution, approved quotations, and context—not a publisher article to rewrite. Daily stories must answer what happened, what is confirmed, who says what, why it matters, and what remains uncertain.

    ArtifactVersioned original story draft linked to its claims.

  11. Not built

    Verify the draft

    Fail closed on unsupported or derivative prose.

    Required checks include claim support, factual fidelity, attribution, quotation accuracy, source-text similarity, freshness, and risk-specific warnings. A generated draft is not a verified story.

    ArtifactVerification report attached to a specific story version.

  12. Not built

    Human editorial review

    Inspect evidence and approve consequential decisions.

    The official publication requires human approval at launch. The future editorial PWA must support cluster merge/split/reject decisions, claim-and-evidence review, passage editing, warnings, story approval, edition assembly, and correction handling.

    ArtifactAppend-only EditorialDecision and approval.

  13. Not built

    Assemble the finite edition

    Fit approved stories to the product promise.

    The Top Ten targets ten stories and 10–15 minutes. The Complete Edition targets about 45 minutes. A slow day is not padded, and an important day does not become an endless feed.

    ArtifactCanonical Edition containing immutable story versions.

  14. Not built

    Render & publish

    Create every delivery format from one approved record.

    Web, RSS, email, printable PDF, and EPUB must render from the same canonical edition. Renderers cannot rewrite reporting. Publication fails closed if evidence, approval, or any required artifact is missing.

    ArtifactFormat Artifact and auditable Delivery records.

  15. Not built

    Evaluate, update & correct

    Measure quality and preserve accountable history.

    Benchmark ranking and clustering decisions, monitor source and model drift, and review misses. Corrections create new story and edition history and propagate to every affected format; published text is never silently replaced.

    ArtifactEvaluation set, audit events, and visible Correction records.

Architecture boundary

Models advise; records decide.

Deterministic code controls source eligibility, filtering, deduplication, schemas, and publication gates. Models handle bounded editorial tasks, and their outputs remain inspectable inputs—not silent authority.

InputPermitted sourcesPolicies, collection metadata, snapshots
ReportingEvents & evidenceClusters, dossiers, claims, uncertainty
EditorialStories & approvalScores, decisions, versions, edition
OutputOne edition, five formatsWeb, RSS, email, PDF, EPUB
Non-negotiable boundary: full article text may inform transient analysis under its source policy, but the writing stage receives verified claims and evidence references. The repository must not contain collected publisher text.

What has been demonstrated

Current evidence

The first live experiment answers a narrow question: can the system make a bounded dossier from a real event and obtain a machine-valid editorial score?

Calibre corpus

Ten-source extraction run

Maintained Calibre recipes retrieved complete configured editions and the collector normalized every extracted item against one schema.

  • Thirty-three items were under 150 words, mainly video, player, cartoon, or social entries.

  • A PBS qualification exposed malformed struct_time serialization; the bridge now emits RFC 3339 and Rails strictly rejects non-ISO dates instead of guessing.

Automated checks

Prototype verification

  • The expanded Rails suite passes with 39 tests and 201 assertions.
  • The Calibre bridge passes 15 Python tests, including a real Calibre 9.11 no-repeat network test.
  • Ten changed scoring files passed the configured RuboCop checks.
  • The scorer rejects invalid fields and out-of-range score values.
  • The local dossier tests check per-publisher diversity, substantial singleton admission, minimum-body rejection, and deterministic evidence selection.

These checks validate the prototype’s mechanics. They do not establish editorial accuracy across many story types.

Known limitations

Not yet proven

  • Independent cluster precision and recall across the fixed Calibre corpus.
  • Content-level syndication detection across different domains.
  • Score consistency across sections, countries, and story types.
  • A ranking formula that produces a balanced finite edition.
  • Claim extraction, contradiction handling, original drafting, verification, or publication.
  • Per-publisher production collection and retention approval.
  • Exact per-request Calibre HTTP-status and final-redirect instrumentation.
  • Rails subprocess orchestration, pinned Calibre image packaging, and the enabled hourly coordinator.
  • Human precision and missed-merge labels for the 53 stricter clustering proposals.

Immediate agenda

Next decisions

Progress remains deliberately bounded. Each decision should be discussed, implemented, and tested before moving the system’s working edge forward.

Locked

Use anchored ranking dimensions

Consequence, audience relevance, geographic reach, public interest, and novelty each have observable 0–5 anchors. Continue testing them on dissimilar events for drift and ambiguity.

Locked

Use idempotent versioned storage

Canonical URLs identify articles, content hashes identify immutable versions, and observations preserve every run and section appearance. Publication dates retain their source and confidence.

Locked

Use cutoff-to-cutoff continuity

Use 2:00 AM Eastern edition cutoffs, a Friday-to-Monday window for Monday, and persistent clusters that are rescored only when new evidence arrives. Preserve substantial singletons as candidates.

Now · HITL

Qualify the US source roster

Run one publisher at a time through the shared manifest, no-repeat, date, extraction-quality, and policy gates. Only then connect approved sources to the hourly Solid Queue coordinator.
